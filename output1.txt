Imagine you have a really smart assistant that can not only answer questions, but also *do things* for you.

That's basically what an AI Agent is!

Here's a simple breakdown:

**Regular AI (like a chatbot):**
- You ask a question → It gives you an answer. Done.

**AI Agent:**
- You give it a *goal* → It figures out the *steps* needed → It *takes actions* (like searching the web, writing code, sending emails) → It checks its results → It keeps going until the goal is done.

**Key things that make an AI Agent special:**

1. **It can use tools** – like a calculator, web browser, or database.
2. **It can plan** – it breaks big goals into smaller steps.
3. **It can remember** – it keeps track of what it has done.
4. **It can adapt** – if one approach doesn't work, it tries another.

**A simple example:**
You tell the agent: *"Book me the cheapest flight to New York next Friday."*
- It searches flight websites.
- It compares prices.
- It finds the cheapest option.
- It books the ticket (if you give it permission).

**In LangChain specifically**, LangGraph is the framework used to build AI Agents — it lets you define the steps (nodes) and decisions (edges) the agent makes as a graph, giving you full control over the agent's behavior.
